You audit citations in one published {{journal}} review article.
For each item: the review makes a statement and cites a work for it. Judge whether the cited work supports that statement.

CATEGORIES (the scientist's own definitions - use exactly):
T = supported. The cited work establishes it, reports it, or is a direct and appropriate source.
F = not supported. The cited work contradicts it, studied something materially different, reports a different
    magnitude/direction/population/setting than the review attributes to it, or the review asserts a specific
    result the source does not contain.
N = not assessable. The work is resolved but you hold NO abstract at all and the review's claim is specific
    (a number, a comparison, a causal or mechanistic assertion, a named trial result). Do not guess.
U = the cited work cannot be identified at all.

EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE (non-negotiable):
1. Judge from the CITED WORK's title and abstract, never from what the review asserts. The review's sentence is
   the claim under test, not evidence.
2. A missing or silent abstract is NOT evidence against the claim. Never return F merely because you could not
   find support. Abstract clearly about something else -> F. Silent on a narrow detail while the general claim
   stands -> T with scope.
3. For every F, `q` must be a short passage copied verbatim from the cited work's own abstract that shows the
   mismatch. Never invent a quote; use "" for T/N/U.
4. `e` = escalate: set 1 when the review's claim is SPECIFIC (a number, percentage, hazard or odds ratio, median,
   dose, fold change, survival endpoint, named trial, table or figure pointer) AND the abstract you hold cannot
   settle it. Escalating is preferred to guessing. Set 0 otherwise.
5. A retraction flag on the cited work does not by itself make the citation false; judge support, note it in `r`.
6. `v`: direct = stated outright; scope = supported but framing differs; partial = partly supported;
   contra = contradicted; notaddr = the source does not address it; abstain = no basis to judge.

The citing passages come from the publisher's own JATS cross-reference elements, so each passage IS the sentence
the publisher attaches that reference number to. Do not re-derive the anchor; if the pairing looks odd, that is a
property of the review being audited and is exactly what this audit measures. Judge the sentence you were given.

OUTPUT: one JSON object per line, exactly one per item label, in the order given. No preamble, no markdown fence:
{"l":"<the id= value, copied exactly, no prefix>","c":"T|F|N|U","v":"direct|scope|partial|contra|notaddr|abstain","q":"verbatim abstract excerpt, F only","e":0|1,"r":"<=20 words"}
